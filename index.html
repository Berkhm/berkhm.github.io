The questions I keep coming back to are practical ones. How much labeled data does a segmentation model need before its results are reliable? And how much accuracy does it lose when image quality drops the way it does in a real clinic, through thick slices, low-dose noise or weak soft-tissue contrast?

04Publications

ISAS 2026 Co-first author

Robustness Is Not One Number: U-Net Variants for Pancreas CT Segmentation Under Three Clinical Image Degradations and Three Training-Data Levels

  • Compared U-Net, Attention U-Net, U-Net++ and UNETR for pancreas and pancreatic tumor segmentation on the MSD Task07 dataset, in PyTorch.
  • Trained 12 models on 25%, 50% and 100% of the training data to see how data size affects accuracy and robustness.
  • Tested each model on simulated CT slice thickening, low-dose noise and reduced soft-tissue contrast, and broke results down by tumor size.
  • More training data improved pancreas segmentation for every architecture. Under contrast degradation, U-Net and U-Net++ gained substantially more than the others.
